## Teaching: Concept and Objectives

1.1 Meaning of Teaching
- Teaching: A purposeful, planned and interactive process through which a teacher facilitates learning.
- It involves the transfer of knowledge, development of skills, attitudes and values.
- Teaching is a tripolar process involving Teacher, Learner and Curriculum.
- Teaching is both an art and a science.
Important characteristics:
- Goal-oriented and purposeful.
- Interactive and social process.
- Both formal and informal.
- Continuous and dynamic.
- Involves communication between teacher and learner.
- Encourages behavioural changes.
- Based on psychological and educational principles.
1.2 Objectives of Teaching
The major objectives of teaching are:
## Knowledge: Acquisition of facts, concepts and information.
## Understanding: Developing comprehension of concepts.
## Application: Using acquired knowledge in practical situations.
## Skill development: Developing intellectual, technical and practical skills.
## Attitude development: Developing positive attitudes and values.
## Critical thinking: Developing analytical and problem-solving abilities.
## Creativity: Encouraging innovation and original thinking.
## Personality development: Developing social and emotional maturity.
1.3 Domains of Educational Objectives – Bloom's Taxonomy
Bloom's Taxonomy (**1956**) classifies educational objectives into three domains.

- Cognitive Domain (Knowledge and Intellectual Skills)

Revised Bloom's Taxonomy by Anderson and Krathwohl (**2001**):
Level	Meaning	Example
## Remember	Recall information	Define teaching
## Understand	Explain concepts	Explain learning
## Apply	Use knowledge	Solve a problem
## Analyze	Break into parts	Compare theories
## Evaluate	Make judgments	Critically examine
## Create	Produce something new	Design a teaching plan

Important: Revised cognitive hierarchy

Remember → Understand → Apply → Analyze → Evaluate → Create.

- Affective Domain (Attitudes and Values)

Developed by Krathwohl, Bloom and Masia (**1964**).
Level	Meaning
## Receiving	Willingness to listen
## Responding	Active participation
## Valuing	Accepting and appreciating values
## Organization	Organizing values
## Characterization	Values become part of personality

- Psychomotor Domain (Physical and Motor Skills)

Associated with Simpson (**1972**) and other classification frameworks.
- Perception
- Set
- Guided Response
- Mechanism
- Complex Overt Response
- Adaptation
- Origination
**PYQ** Focus
- Cognitive → Knowledge and thinking.
- Affective → Attitudes and values.
- Psychomotor → Physical and motor skills.
- Bloom (**1956**) → Original cognitive taxonomy.
- Anderson and Krathwohl (**2001**) → Revised taxonomy.

## Levels of Teaching

There are three major levels of teaching:
## Memory Level
## Understanding Level
## Reflective Level
2.1 Memory Level of Teaching
- Associated with Johann Friedrich Herbart.
- Lowest level of teaching.
- Emphasizes memorization and recall of information.
- Teacher plays a dominant role.
- Learners reproduce previously learned information.
- Focuses on rote learning.
Characteristics:
- Focus on memorization.
- Teacher-centred approach.
- Minimum learner participation.
- Suitable for factual knowledge.
- Evaluation through recall-based questions.
Example: Memorizing definitions, formulas, dates and historical events.
2.2 Understanding Level of Teaching
- Associated with Morris L. Bigge.
- Higher than memory level.
- Focuses on understanding relationships between facts and concepts.
- Encourages comprehension and interpretation.
- Learners understand the meaning of what they study.
Characteristics:
- Focus on comprehension.
- Explains cause-and-effect relationships.
- Develops reasoning abilities.
- Encourages comparison and classification.
- Teacher acts as a guide and facilitator.
Example: Understanding why an algorithm has a particular time complexity rather than memorizing its definition.
2.3 Reflective Level of Teaching
- Associated with Hunt.
- Highest level of teaching.
- Focuses on critical thinking and problem-solving.
- Learners independently analyze problems.
- Encourages creativity and independent judgment.
Characteristics:
- Learner-centred approach.
- Problem-solving orientation.
- Critical and reflective thinking.
- Independent learning.
- Open-ended questions.
- Teacher acts as a facilitator.
Example: Asking students to design an efficient solution for a real-world problem and justify their approach.
2.4 Comparison of Three Levels
Feature	Memory	Understanding	Reflective
Thinker	Herbart	Bigge	Hunt
Level	Lowest	Middle	Highest
Focus	Recall	Comprehension	Critical thinking
Approach	Teacher-centred	More interactive	Learner-centred
Learner role	Passive	Active	Highly active
Thinking	Recall	Reasoning	Problem-solving
Evaluation	Reproduction	Explanation	Critical analysis

### Very Important

Hierarchy:

Memory → Understanding → Reflective
- Memory: What?
- Understanding: Why and how?
- Reflective: What if? How can we solve it?

3.3 Maxims of Teaching
Maxims are general principles that help teachers organize instruction effectively.
Maxim	Meaning
Known to Unknown	Start with familiar concepts
Simple to Complex	Teach easy concepts before difficult ones
Concrete to Abstract	Begin with real objects
Particular to General	Derive general rules from examples
General to Particular	Apply general principles to specific cases
Whole to Part	Introduce the complete concept first
Analysis to Synthesis	Break down and then combine concepts
Empirical to Rational	Move from experience to reasoning

Example: Teaching multiplication using physical objects before introducing abstract mathematical operations.

## Evaluation Systems

8.1 Meaning of Evaluation Evaluation is the systematic process of determining the extent to which educational objectives have been achieved.

Important distinction:
Concept	Meaning
Measurement	Quantitative description
Assessment	Collecting information about learning
Evaluation	Making judgments based on evidence

Example:
- Measurement: Student scores 80 marks.
- Assessment: Identifying strengths and weaknesses.
- Evaluation: Judging whether learning objectives were achieved.
8.2 Elements of Evaluation
## Learning objectives.
## Learning experiences.
## Assessment techniques.
## Collection of evidence.
## Interpretation of results.
## Feedback.
## Improvement and decision-making.
8.3 Types of Evaluation
- Formative Evaluation
- Conducted during the teaching-learning process.
- Helps identify learning difficulties.
- Provides continuous feedback.
- Improves teaching and learning.
Examples: Class tests, quizzes, assignments and classroom discussions.

- Summative Evaluation
- Conducted at the end of a course or instructional period.
- Measures overall achievement.
- Usually used for grading and certification.
Examples: Final examinations and semester examinations.

- Diagnostic Evaluation
- Identifies specific learning difficulties.
- Finds the causes of learning problems.
- Helps teachers plan remedial instruction.
- Placement Evaluation
- Determines the learner's existing knowledge and abilities.
- Helps place learners at an appropriate instructional level.
8.4 Comparison of Evaluation Types
Type	Purpose	Timing
Placement	Determine entry-level ability	Before instruction
Formative	Improve learning	During instruction
Diagnostic	Identify learning difficulties	When difficulties arise
Summative	Measure achievement	End of instruction

8.5 Norm-Referenced vs Criterion-Referenced Evaluation
Norm-Referenced	Criterion-Referenced
Compares learners with others	Compares learners with fixed standards
Relative performance	Absolute performance against criteria
Ranking-oriented	Mastery-oriented
Example: Competitive ranking	Example: Pass mark of 40%

8.6 Continuous and Comprehensive Evaluation (**CCE**)
- Continuous: Assessment throughout the academic year.
- Comprehensive: Covers scholastic and co-scholastic development.
- Focuses on overall development.
- Reduces dependence on a single final examination.
